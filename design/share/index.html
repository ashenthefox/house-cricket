The pitch

You are Pip, a house cricket bought from a pet store to feed a leopard gecko. On the night you escape the tank, the feeder cup tips over and your cupmates scatter through the house. Ten of them are caught in ten rooms, each guarded by a big, polite, very hungry gecko.

Explore a hand-painted watercolor house as one connected map. Follow each trapped cricket's real chirp, which gets louder as you get closer. Find household items that let you reach new places. Outwit each gecko without ever hurting it. Every friend you save moves into the Cricket Carrier, which grows from a bare plastic box into a little cricket town. And remember: almost everything in this house that chirps is not a cricket.

It's a large game: 10 zones and about 70 rooms. The main path takes about an hour and a half, and finding everything takes 2 to 3 hours. It runs in a web browser on computers and phones (held sideways), and plays start to finish with a PS5 or other controller.

The story

"Every Saturday, the pet store sells crickets by the cup. This cup was for Duchess. Pip had other plans."

The game opens with three quick panels: the pet-store shelf, the car ride, and the kid's bedroom with Duchess the leopard gecko in her tank. Then you take control. Dodge the tongs inside the cup, leap out, and run past a half-asleep Duchess, who teaches you the tell every gecko shares: the tail tip wiggles, with a soft rattle, just before a strike. Climb the tank decor and squeeze out through the lid. Your leap knocks the cup over, and everyone scatters into the dark.

Right away you chirp for the first time, and someone answers. Stripe, your first friend to rescue, is out there.

The ending: you've sung giant Nana to sleep in the attic. The kid finds the Carrier full of crickets, and the geckos get fruit-mix gecko food instead (a real thing that crested geckos love). The Carrier, now a little cricket town, goes onto the sunny windowsill, and the crickets choose to stay, singing.

The tone is gentle humor, no cruelty, and nobody gets eaten on screen. The kid is only ever shown as hands, a sleeve and sneakers.

How it plays

Three cricket verbs: Chirp, Jump, Hide. Chirp to call, stun and find. Jump everywhere. Hide in crack-sized spaces nothing bigger can reach.

Moment to moment: run, hop, wall-jump and swing through a room. Kick a bug out of the way. Stop, raise your front legs to Listen (crickets really do hear through their legs) and see which way the chirp is coming from.

A zone, about 10 to 15 minutes: enter a new room of the house. Follow the trapped cricket's call: it first carries through a vent or crack beside the zone's item, then leads on to the gecko's lair. Find the item, outwit the gecko, free the cricket, and pop home to see the Carrier grow.

Why you keep going: each new item opens secrets in rooms you've already seen. The Carrier's empty spots show who's still missing. The attic only opens once all nine other crickets are home, so the finale is always the whole chorus.

The two things the game lives or dies by: hopping should feel springy and precise, like a cricket landing with weight, and following a chirp should feel like real detective work by ear. The beacon never goes silent and has an on-screen ripple option for playing with the sound off.

Hide

Crickets fit where nothing else does. Press up beside a baseboard crack, a bottle cap, a sock fold or a gap between book spines to tuck in. Enemies and geckos can't see or reach you there, though chirping gives you away to anything that hunts by ear. A few things can't be beaten at all: plodding darkling beetles and the robot vacuum. For those you hide, let them pass, and go on. Sneak rooms are kept rare (one or two per zone at most, never a timed chase), and hiding shows up in a few boss fights too.

Following the chirp, your way

A small chirp indicator at the screen's edge points toward the trapped cricket and pulses with each chirp. It's on by default. The Beacon chirps setting chooses whether the cricket sings Always, only When I call (it answers your own Chirp button), or is Off (the indicator alone). The beacon has its own volume slider, and your own Chirp button always works and always gets an answer.

Forgiving by design

A fall costs one seed-heart and puts you back on the last solid ground. There are no lives and no game over. Boss fights restart at the current phase, not the start. An optional Gentle Mode halves damage and slows the gecko tells.

The first three zones run in order as the tutorial. After the Kitchen you choose the Pantry or the Laundry first, and the Living Room has two ways in.

The crickets

Every rescued cricket is playable. You travel with a trio and swap between them at any time, even mid-fight. Stats run from 1 to 5. Perks only open optional secrets, so swapping is always a choice, never a chore.

Each one is a real species, drawn with its real shape and colors and given its real song. Only male crickets sing, so Pip is male. Hump the camel cricket is wingless and really is silent, so he drums instead. Mote really is that small, and Duo really is that big.

The geckos

Scary but friendly. Each one is nearly a screen long, with glittering eyes and a mouth full of manners, and is sincerely sorry that you look delicious. Every one warns you the same way before it strikes: the tail tip wiggles, with a soft rattle (real gecko hunting behavior). Win, and they're good sports ("Fair's fair, little one"); afterwards you'll find them dozing around the house. Most geckos have no eyelids, so they never blink and lick their eyes clean instead. Lashes does it all through every conversation.

The ten fights

  1. Duchess, leopard gecko (Bedroom): avoid. Make her lunge into the sand three times. While her snout is stuck, run up her back to free Stripe.
  2. Chikchak, house gecko (Bathroom): stealth. She chirps to lure you. Hide in the steam clouds and the grout cracks, turn three faucets to flood her ledge, then swing to Katy.
  3. Big Tok, tokay (Kitchen): distract. Jump his "TO-KAY!" shockwaves. Kick a cork, a spoon and a sponge for him to chomp, then raft to Duo while his jaw is busy.
  4. Lashes, crested gecko (Pantry): lure with fruit. Drop a grape, jam and a banana in his path and he stops to lick them. Every time he licks his eye, he isn't looking. Chirp the fruit flies away.
  5. Peridot, giant day gecko (Laundry): put her to sleep. Day geckos doze in the dark. Glide to three light pull-chains until she naps, and find Hump under the smoke alarm.
  6. Gargy, gargoyle gecko (Living room): stun. Ground-pound the cushions to bounce a throw pillow into him. Three soft bonks and he sulks off.
  7. Mossy, leaf-tailed gecko (Study): hide and seek. He camouflages among the books. Chirp to make his eyes shine back, then tag him during the glint.
  8. Kite, flying gecko (Sunroom): chase. He glides off with Digby's leaf. Race him through the canopy and chirp to startle him lower.
  9. Ember, cave gecko (Basement): dazzle. She hunts by sound in the dark, so hide and stay silent. Flash your glow bead when her red eyes come close.
  10. Nana, giant gecko (Attic): the lullaby. Duck into rafter gaps from her tail sweeps and light three music stones. Match each stone with a low, middle or high cricket voice. Then everyone you've rescued sings her to sleep together, and Chip, Pip's cupmate, is free.

The Not-Crickets

The running joke you asked for. Each of these is first heard as a false beacon, a chirp you follow thinking it's a friend, and each one ends with "I'm not a cricket!" Then each helps you, moves into the Carrier, and sings in the finale.

The puzzle side: Listen draws the sound's rhythm as a strip of pulses. A real cricket is short, even pulse groups. A smoke alarm is one beep and a long wait. A tree frog is one rising peep. A swift is a messy rattle. The Study's beacon maze depends on telling them apart.

About "chimney sparrow": the bird that nests in chimneys and chitters is the chimney swift. Her old folk names include chimney swallow and even chimney bat, so being called a sparrow (and now a cricket) is her running complaint.

The enemies

The house is full of critters that get in your way. Kick or chirp them and they flip over, scurry off and come back later; nobody gets squashed. Each room brings one or two new ones. (The friendly ones are the exceptions: Page is the one silverfish who reads instead of nibbling, Sgt. Crumb is an ant who quit, and Lumen is a big fuzzy moth, not a clothes moth.)

Can't be beaten: hide and let them pass

The Dragon's Lair

Behind the basement furnace, an old glass vivarium glows under a hot basking lamp. Inside is a hoard: every button, bottle cap, coin, marble and candy wrapper the house ever lost. Its keeper is Sir Bartholomew, a bearded dragon. He is not a gecko, and he takes that very seriously.

He's far too big for the screen. You only ever see parts of him: a golden eye in the gloom, a clawed foot, the spiky beard turning black when he spots you. You skitter between the piles and hide under bottle caps while he head-bobs and searches. Chirping gives you away.

Reach the top of the hoard, stand in plain view, and wave. That slow arm-wave is how real bearded dragons say "I'm no threat." He stops, waves back, and his beard pales. "…Nobody ever waves."

He gives you the Cellophane Cloak, a crinkly clear candy wrapper from the hoard. Wear it to turn nearly invisible for a few seconds, enough to slip past the beetles and the vacuum in the open. It's optional, and it opens shortcuts and secrets. (You suggested an invisibility ring. The wrapper does the same job at cricket scale, without borrowing a famous story's ring.) He can't fit in the Carrier, so he sends a gift: a button throne and a marble.

The Cricket Carrier

The pet-store cricket keeper the kid left on the bedroom floor becomes home. It changes in four big stages: a bare box, then moss and a second level, then string lights and a ladder, then rooftops and a garden. Every one of the 23 arrivals adds something you can see.

Each rescued cricket brings a piece: Stripe's chalk running track, Rin's bamboo bell, Hump's bottle-cap drum. Each cricket's song also joins the Carrier's music, so home sounds fuller with every rescue. At the ninth rescue they sing together for the first time, and the sound shakes open the attic.

Every save crack takes you home from the very start. After each rescue, the game offers to take you there to watch the newcomer move in.

Optional recruits (the Suikoden part)

Eight critters you find by exploring. Each one moves in and opens something new in the Carrier.

Look and sound

Art: soft storybook watercolor with visible paper grain, loose washes that bleed at the edges, and sepia ink on the characters. Each room has its own four-color palette (above). The backgrounds stay pale and hazy, so the platforms, always the darkest shapes, never get confused with scenery.

Everything shown here is a real early picture made for this game. Pip is the reference for every other character, and the bedroom painting is the reference for every other room, so it all looks like one game.

Music: small chamber instruments, a different mood per room. Felt piano and harp for the bedroom, bouncy bassoon for the kitchen, low drones and drips for the basement. Melodies stay low so they never mask a chirp.

The chirps, from research

Real crickets sing by rubbing a scraper on one wing along a toothed file on the other, and each wing closing makes one pulse. The game builds every song from these real figures, so each friend sounds like its species:

Size of the job

A large game. About 70 rooms in 10 zones, plus the tank, the Carrier, the hall, the stairwell and the chimney.

  • Characters: 11 playable crickets, 10 gecko fights of 3 phases each, an optional bearded-dragon fight, 18 kinds of enemy plus 2 you can't beat, 4 Not-Crickets and 8 recruits. Almost all of them talk.
  • Art: about 200 pictures in all, mostly small sprites and props, and 8 story panels. Pip's movements are animated, and every cricket gets a sheet of poses.
  • Sound: 14 music tracks, about 22 recorded sound effects, and synthesized songs for every cricket and every sound that fools you.

The hardest parts:

  • 70 rooms that are fun and correctly gated. Automatic checks prove every route works.
  • Ten distinct boss fights.
  • Movement and animation that feel great: chirp, jump and hide are the heart of it, and every loop gets reworked until it reads at small size.
  • Ten gecko species that look like one family.
  • A chirp beacon that stays clear on phone speakers.

It fits within the budget set for art and sound, with room for retries.

What would help most

  • Is the look right? Every character and enemy on this page is a mock picture, and together they set the style for the rest of the game.
  • Does hiding feel like the right amount? Should the Dragon's Lair be optional, or part of the main path?
  • Does each cricket feel like its own character? Who's your favorite?
  • Do the gecko fights sound fun, and gentle enough?
  • Are the Not-Crickets funny? Any other sound-alikes you'd add (a squeaky shoe, a phone on vibrate, a wind-up toy)?
  • Names: Pip, Chip, Duchess, Big Tok, Nana, and the rest. Any you'd change?
  • Is the size right: 10 zones, about 2 to 3 hours?
